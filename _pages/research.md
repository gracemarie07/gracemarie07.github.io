<div class="page-lead"><p class="eyebrow">RESEARCH</p><h1>Reading the physics of cosmic explosions in light.</h1><p>I combine spectroscopy, time-domain surveys, and computational analysis to study how transient objects evolve.</p></div>

<section class="research-project" id="sn-2025aico"><div class="project-number">01</div><div><p class="card-kicker">SUPERNOVAE · OPTICAL/NIR SPECTROSCOPY · JWST</p><h2>SN 2025aico: radioactive mixing, ejecta asymmetry, and dust</h2><p>During an NSF REU at the University of Hawaiʻi Institute for Astronomy, I conducted remote observations with the Keck and NASA Infrared Telescope Facilities and analyzed an unusually broad optical-to-mid-infrared dataset for the Type IIb supernova SN 2025aico.</p><p>The spectral sequence follows the event from 1.64 to 166.82 days after explosion. Its near-infrared helium lines show a distinctive three-stage velocity evolution that favors limited outward mixing of radioactive nickel. At late times, matching double-peaked helium and oxygen profiles reveal coherent asymmetry across the inner ejecta. Serendipitous JWST/MIRI observations additionally provide an early look at warm and cool dust emission.</p><ul class="tag-list"><li>Keck</li><li>NASA IRTF</li><li>JWST/MIRI</li><li>Python</li><li>Spectral analysis</li></ul></div></section>

<section class="research-project"><div class="project-number">02</div><div><p class="card-kicker">TIME-DOMAIN ASTRONOMY · AGN · SURVEY SCIENCE</p><h2>Searching for changing-look active galactic nuclei</h2><p>With the Penn State Department of Astronomy and Astrophysics, I analyze Zwicky Transient Facility and Rubin Observatory survey data to identify unusual variability. I develop Python pipelines that track photometric behavior and help distinguish changing-look active galactic nuclei from other transient sources.</p><ul class="tag-list"><li>ZTF</li><li>Rubin Observatory</li><li>Photometry</li><li>Python pipelines</li></ul></div></section>
